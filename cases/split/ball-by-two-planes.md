A ball of radius 5 centered on the origin, and two sheets: the planes z = −2 and z = 2, each a
square from (−10, −10) to (10, 10) in its plane, wider than the ball.

Split the ball with both sheets at once, keeping all three pieces: a cap below z = −2, a slab
between the planes and a cap above z = 2, each with its own flat face, a disk of radius √21, where
a sheet cut it. Then move the top cap up by 10 and the bottom cap down by 10, along z, so that no
two pieces touch.

The result is three bodies, which together hold the whole ball: the cap of the ball of radius 5
about the origin above z = 2, moved up to lie above z = 12; the slab of that ball between z = −2
and z = 2; and its cap below z = −2, moved down to lie below z = −12.
